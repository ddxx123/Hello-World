Anatomical Terminology Study App

Reference sheet based on OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, section 1.6, to help learners review position, direction, planes, and body regions.

Anatomical Position

The standard anatomical position describes a person standing upright, feet at shoulder width and parallel, toes pointing forward, arms at the sides, and palms facing forward.

  • Reference posture
  • Palms anterior
  • Neutral orientation

Directional Terms

Superior / Inferior
Superior means toward the head; inferior means away from the head.
Anterior / Posterior
Anterior means toward the front; posterior means toward the back.
Medial / Lateral
Medial means toward the midline; lateral means away from the midline.
Proximal / Distal
Proximal means closer to the trunk or point of origin; distal means farther away.
Superficial / Deep
Superficial means close to body surface; deep means farther from the surface.

Body Planes

Plane How it divides the body Example idea
Sagittal Left and right portions (midsagittal is equal halves). Comparing right and left cerebral hemispheres.
Frontal (Coronal) Anterior and posterior portions. Viewing chest structures from the front.
Transverse Superior and inferior portions. Cross-sectional abdominal imaging.

Abdominopelvic Regions and Quadrants

The nine-region method uses right/left hypochondriac, epigastric, right/left lumbar, umbilical, right/left iliac (inguinal), and hypogastric (pubic) regions. Clinically, the four quadrants are RUQ, LUQ, RLQ, and LLQ.

  • RUQ
  • LUQ
  • RLQ
  • LLQ

Major Body Cavities

Dorsal cavity
Contains cranial cavity and vertebral (spinal) cavity.
Ventral cavity
Contains thoracic cavity and abdominopelvic cavity, separated by the diaphragm.
Serous membranes
Pleura surrounds lungs, pericardium surrounds heart, and peritoneum lines abdominopelvic organs.
